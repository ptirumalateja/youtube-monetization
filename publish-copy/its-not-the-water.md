## YouTube Shorts
Title: Why do your fingers wrinkle in water? It's not the water 🤯 #shorts

Description:
Wrinkly bath fingers aren't waterlogged skin. It's an active response controlled by your nervous system.

About 5 minutes in warm water, nerves signal the blood vessels in your fingertips to narrow (vasoconstriction). The fingertip pad loses a little volume and the skin buckles into ridges. If the nerve to a finger is damaged, it doesn't wrinkle, which is why doctors have used the "immersion wrinkle test" to check nerve function.

Why would we have this? The leading idea is grip: the ridges channel water away like treads on a tyre. One 2013 study found people moved wet objects faster with wrinkled fingers; a 2014 study didn't replicate it, so scientists still debate it.

## Facts and sources
- Wrinkling is nerve-driven (sympathetic vasoconstriction in the fingertip), not passive swelling. Lewis & Pickering 1935 (Clinical Science) showed fingers with a cut median nerve don't wrinkle; the immersion wrinkle test is used clinically for nerve function (e.g. Wilder-Smith, 2004).
- Onset after about 5 minutes in warm water.
- Grip: Kareklas, Nettle & Smulders 2013, Biology Letters (wrinkled fingers handled wet objects faster); Haseleu et al. 2014, PLOS ONE (no effect). The VO says "Scientists think" for that reason.
- Visuals are a stylised 3D illustration (not to scale; vessels and nerves simplified).
