### Pinned comment (all platforms)
Why it only works from long range: as the ball slows down, the spin's sideways push stays strong compared with its speed, so the curve keeps tightening (a "spinning ball spiral"). From 20-25 m a free kick bends in a gentle arc. From 35 m it hooks. Source: Dupeux, Le Goff, Quéré & Clanet, New Journal of Physics 12, 093004 (2010).

## Facts and sources

- 3 June 1997, opening match of Le Tournoi, Stade de Gerland in Lyon. Brazil 1-1 France, and the free kick came in the 21st minute. Left foot, struck with the outside of the boot (Robert Pires' account). A ball boy ducked, it clipped the post on its way in, and Barthez stayed motionless ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/10899358/roberto-carlos-brilliant-brazil-free-kick-against-france-remembered), [Wikipedia: 1997 Tournoi de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Tournoi_de_France)).
- Distance: "about 35 m" (ScienceDaily and the NJP paper). Wikipedia gives 33.13 m, so the copy says "about 35 metres".
- Physics: Dupeux, Le Goff, Quéré, Clanet, "The spinning ball spiral", New Journal of Physics 12, 093004 (2010), [doi:10.1088/1367-2630/12/9/093004](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/12/9/093004). Long range is a necessary condition for the spiral, and shorter free kicks (Beckham, Platini, 20-25 m) follow near-circular arcs ([ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/09/100902073509.htm)).
- In the simulation, the lift coefficient is proportional to the spin parameter (Rω/v). That makes the sideways push scale with speed while the turning radius scales with speed, so the curve tightens as the ball slows. That is the paper's mechanism. Drag Cd is 0.25, the ball is 0.43 kg with a 0.11 m radius, and the spin axis is near-vertical. The red "expected" line is the same strike with no spin (drag and gravity only), which finishes several metres wide of the post.
- Stylised on purpose: silhouette figures, the stadium is generic (not Gerland), the slow motion, rewind and freeze are for readability, and the airflow lines are a 2D flow diagram, not a CFD result.
