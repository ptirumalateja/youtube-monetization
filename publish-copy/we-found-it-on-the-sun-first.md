> In 1868 a total eclipse crossed India. At Guntur, the French astronomer Pierre Janssen pointed a spectroscope at the Sun's edge and saw a bright yellow line that was not sodium.
> The English astronomer Norman Lockyer saw the same line, and both reports reached the Paris Academy of Sciences on 26 October 1868. The element was named helium, from the Greek for the Sun.

## Facts and sources

- Total eclipse of 18 Aug 1868; Janssen observed from Guntur, Madras Presidency, British India; bright yellow line (about 587.5 nm), not sodium. Source: [Wikipedia, Solar eclipse of 18 August 1868](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_August_18,_1868).
- Lockyer independently found it; both findings were presented to the Académie des sciences on 26 Oct 1868 (same source).
- Frankland and Lockyer proposed the name helium; Ramsay isolated it in 1895 from cleveite. Source: [Purdue chemistry history, helium](https://www.chem.purdue.edu/history/helium.html).
- 1895 - 1868 = 27 years. Hydrogen lines shown: 656.3, 486.1, 434.0, 410.2 nm.
- Caveat: an 1881 sighting at Vesuvius (Palmieri) was never confirmed, so the reel says "nobody had held it", not "nobody had seen it".
