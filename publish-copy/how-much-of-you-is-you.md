## Facts and sources

- Sender R, Fuchs S, Milo R (2016). Revised estimates for the number of human and bacteria cells in the body. *PLoS Biology* 14(8): e1002533. For a 70 kg, 20-30 year old reference man: about 3.0 x 10^13 human cells and 3.8 x 10^13 bacterial cells (ratio about 1.3 to 1), bacteria about 0.2 kg. These are order-of-magnitude estimates, not exact counts, and they vary between people. The old "10 to 1" claim is outdated.
- "Less than half": 30 / (30 + 38) = about 44% of the cells are human, by cell count only (not by mass, volume or genes). The reel's source line says "By cell count".
- Teaser claim ("the oldest thing in you is older than the Sun"): most of the hydrogen in the body formed in the Big Bang about 13.8 billion years ago; the Sun is about 4.6 billion years old. Verify before posting the follow-up reel.
