**Description**
The snake and the saw is an old parable about overreacting: a snake gets one tiny cut from a saw, decides the saw is attacking him, and destroys himself fighting something that never moved.

The wild horse effect is the same idea. A bat's bite is tiny, but a panicking horse runs until it collapses. The damage comes from the reaction, not the wound. Anger narrows your attention to the "attacker", so every response feels justified while it quietly costs you time, sleep and peace. Most small hurts (a rude comment, a short reply, a small argument) fade in seconds if you don't feed them.

Before you fire back, ask: "will this matter in a week?" If not, let go of the saw.
