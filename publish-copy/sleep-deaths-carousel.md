## Facebook caption
Freddy Krueger was inspired by real deaths. 😨

In the late 1970s and 1980s, young Hmong refugees who had fled the war in Laos began dying in their sleep across America. Families heard gasping or screaming in the night, and by morning the men were gone. Autopsies found nothing. The CDC gave it a name, Sudden Unexpected Nocturnal Death Syndrome, and by the late 1980s it had taken at least 117 lives.

Hmong elders blamed the dab tsog, a night spirit said to sit on sleepers' chests. People everywhere describe that same crushing visit, and doctors call it sleep paralysis: your mind wakes up while your body is still locked in dream sleep. Researcher Shelley Adler argues the spirit didn't kill anyone, but the terror of it, far from home, may have helped. The deaths are still not fully explained.

Wes Craven read the newspaper stories, including one about a boy so afraid of his nightmares that he refused to sleep. When he finally did, he died in the night. In 1984, Craven turned it into A Nightmare on Elm Street.

Have you ever woken up and couldn't move? 👇
