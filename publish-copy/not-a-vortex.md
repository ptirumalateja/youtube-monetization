## Facts and sources

- Sun's speed around the galaxy about 230 km/s (direction from the full solar velocity (11.1, 245, 7.3) km/s): 48.5 AU per year. On the compressed map the Sun moves at a speed that keeps Earth's true stretch (48.5 orbit radii per orbit); inner planets show clear waves, outer paths are nearly straight.
- Ecliptic tilted 60.2° to the galactic plane (asserted by `render.cjs`); the Sun's motion is 60.6° out of the ecliptic, so Earth is ahead of the Sun 50.5% of each orbit (asserted).
- Planet positions at the start of the orrery are for 21 Oct 2026 (J2000 elements and mean longitudes). Planet axes come from the IAU pole coordinates (Venus and Uranus spin backwards, so their axes lean 177° and 98°).
- Sun to the Galactic Centre about 26,700 ly; one galactic year about 230 million years. The spiral arms turn with the Sun during the lap: the Sun sits close to the spiral pattern's corotation radius, so this is a fair simplification.
- Sagittarius A*: about 4.3 million solar masses, drawn as the EHT-style ring and hugely enlarged (its true shadow is far too small to see at galaxy scale).
- Stylised on purpose: distances compressed (radius = 2.2 √a), planet and moon sizes enlarged, moon periods and planet spins slowed or sped up for readability, the galaxy is our own procedural plate.
