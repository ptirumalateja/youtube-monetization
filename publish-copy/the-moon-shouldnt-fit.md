## Facts and sources

- Sun diameter 1,391,400 km ÷ Moon 3,474.8 km = 400.4×. Mean distances 149.6 M km ÷ 384,400 km = 389×, so "about 400×".
- Apparent sizes: Moon 29.5′ to 32.9′, Sun 31.4′ to 32.5′, so at its far points the Moon is too small and the eclipse is annular.
- Moon recession about 3.8 cm/yr (Apollo lunar laser ranging).
- Last total eclipse: commonly quoted as about 600 million years; published estimates run from roughly 0.6 to over 1 billion years.
- Day turns to night: totality darkness, temperature drop, and insects going quiet (Galen et al. 2018 recorded bees stopping their buzz during the 2017 US eclipse).
- 2 Aug 2027 total eclipse, maximum duration 6 min 23 s near Luxor (NASA eclipse catalogue).
- The Earth shot uses real disc-overlap geometry for the Moon's penumbra and umbra; the Moon's flight in the lock-in is real perspective (the Moon grows 400× as it travels from the Sun's distance to its own).
