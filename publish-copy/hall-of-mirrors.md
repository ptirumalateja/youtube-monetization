**Description**
The House of 1000 Mirrors is an old folk tale about a dog who fought his own reflection, and it explains a real psychology effect called hostile attribution bias.

Hostile attribution bias is the habit of reading unclear behaviour as an attack: a late reply, a short tone, a look. Psychologist Kenneth Dodge began studying it in the 1980s, and research links it to more arguments, more broken friendships and more stress. It makes itself come true because people mirror how we treat them. If you go in defensive, they get defensive back.

The fix is one question before you react: "what else could this mean?" They may be busy, tired or distracted. Most of the time it isn't about you.
