## Facts and sources

The reel is a parable and puts no numbers on screen beyond its own story (AGE 20 to 35 while 30 friends become 1, then 35 to 60 with that one). The caption's two science lines:

| Claim in the copy | Source | How to phrase it (do not overclaim) |
|---|---|---|
| "Our circle of contacts is widest in our mid-20s and slowly shrinks after that." | Bhattacharya, K., Ghosh, A., Monsivais, D., Dunbar, R. I. M. & Kaski, K. (2016). Sex differences in social focus across the life cycle in humans. *Royal Society Open Science*, 3(4), 160097. https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160097 | One operator's anonymised call records in one European country for 2007 (about 3.2 million users with known age and sex). The paper: "the maximum number of connections for both males and females occurs at the age of around 25", then the number falls (faster for men), levels off around 45-55 and falls again after 55. These are phone contacts, not "friends": say "circle of contacts" or "network", and say "suggest", not "prove". |
| "The innermost circle, the people you would call in a crisis, is about five." | Zhou, W.-X., Sornette, D., Hill, R. A. & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2005). Discrete hierarchical organization of social group sizes. *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*, 272, 439-444. Review: Dunbar, R. I. M. (2018). The anatomy of friendship. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 22(1), 32-51. | Dunbar's layers are about 5, 15, 50 and 150 people (cumulative), each roughly three times the one inside it. "About five" is an average with wide individual variation. Do not present 150 or 5 as hard limits. |
| "The 30 and the 1 are a story, not a statistic." | (framing) | Keep this line. The reel's 30 friends and the single friend at 60 are not data. |

Caveats for replies: Dunbar's number (150) is debated (e.g. Lindenfors, Wartel & Lind, 2021, *Biology Letters*, argue the confidence interval is far too wide for one number). The mobile-phone result describes active phone contacts in one country in one year, so it is not a lifelong panel of the same people.

Look note: this reel's composition (a long table seen from a side three-quarter angle, 30 friends, the lamp narrowing from the whole table onto two cream friends, chairs pushed in with the glasses left behind, the toast) is ours and deliberately different from @neuronvisuals' "10 best friends at 18" reel (straight down the table, an overhead pendant stack).
