## Facts and sources

The race is a parable: the runners, the car and the timings are story. The one factual claim is in the caption:

| Claim in the copy | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "In an average OECD country it could take four to five generations for children from the poorest 10% of families to reach the average income." | OECD (2018), *A Broken Social Elevator? How to Promote Social Mobility*, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264301085-en . Foreword: "In an 'average OECD country', it could take five generations for children of poor families to reach the average income in their country." Chapter 1, Figure 1.5: "it takes on average four to five generations for the offspring of a low-income family to reach the average income". | Checked against the report's text. The OECD calls these estimates "simulation-based and intended to be illustrative", based on father-son earnings persistence. Say "could take" / "estimates", never "takes". Country figures (India, Denmark) circulate from secondary summaries (Statista); they sit only in the report's chart and were not verified, so the copy does not use them. |

Caveat for replies: the reel does not say effort is pointless. The caption keeps "run anyway"; the point is the unequal start line.
