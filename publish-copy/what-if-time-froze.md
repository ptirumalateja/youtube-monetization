## Facts and sources
- The startle and fear response runs through fast subcortical paths (thalamus → amygdala), before conscious awareness (LeDoux, *The Emotional Brain*, 1996).
- Sympathetic activation and adrenaline raise heart rate, dilate the pupils and release glucose from the liver (standard physiology of the fight-or-flight response).
- Time doesn't slow during fear. People in a 31 m free fall could not read numbers flashed faster than normal, but they remembered the fall as longer. This points to denser memory, not faster perception (Stetson, Fiesta & Eagleman, "Does Time Really Slow Down during a Frightening Event?", PLoS ONE, 2007).
