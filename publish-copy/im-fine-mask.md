## Facts and sources

The reel itself is a parable and makes no factual claim on screen. The caption's claims:

| Claim in the copy | Source | How to phrase it (do not overclaim) |
|---|---|---|
| People who habitually hide what they feel report less positive emotion, less closeness to others and lower well-being. | Gross, J. J. & John, O. P. (2003). Individual differences in two emotion regulation processes: Implications for affect, relationships, and well-being. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 85(2), 348-362. | Correlational (questionnaires, mostly US college students, plus peer ratings). Say "tend to" / "reported"; never "hiding feelings causes depression". |
| (Background for replies) Suppressing emotion in a conversation affects the other person too. | Butler, E. A., Egloff, B., Wilhelm, F. H., Smith, N. C., Erickson, E. A. & Gross, J. J. (2003). The social consequences of expressive suppression. *Emotion*, 3(1), 48-67. | Lab study: partners of suppressors reported less rapport and showed bigger blood-pressure rises. One set of lab studies, not a general law. |
| "Smiling depression" is not a clinical diagnosis. | It is not a diagnosis in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11; it is an informal term (sometimes linked to depression with atypical features or "masked" presentations). | Keep "a common name for", never present it as a diagnosis. |
| "If you're not fine, talk to someone." | General signposting. | No specific helpline named (none verified for every audience). If the owner wants one, add the verified number for the main audience country before posting. |

Caveats for replies: suppression is not always harmful (it can help in the short term, and effects differ across cultures; e.g. Butler, Lee & Gross, 2007, *Emotion* 7(1), 30-48, found the social costs were smaller among people with more Asian-values backgrounds). Do not give medical advice in comments; point people to someone they trust or local services.
