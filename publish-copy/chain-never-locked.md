## Facts and sources

The reel itself is a parable and makes no factual claim on screen (the chain, the post and the 12 years are story). The caption's science claims:

| Claim in the copy | Source | How to phrase it (do not overclaim) |
|---|---|---|
| "In 1967 psychologists called this learned helplessness." | Seligman, M. E. P. & Maier, S. F. (1967). Failure to escape traumatic shock. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 74(1), 1-9. See also Overmier, J. B. & Seligman, M. E. P. (1967), same journal, 74(1), 28-33. | Dogs that had received shocks they could not control later mostly failed to escape shock in a shuttle box by jumping a barrier, while dogs that had controllable shocks or no shocks learned to escape. Say "most", never "all", and do not quote exact counts unless checked against the paper. It was an animal study; do not present it as a study of people. |
| "Fifty years later, the same two researchers revised the idea: giving up is the default; what has to be learned is control." | Maier, S. F. & Seligman, M. E. P. (2016). Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience. *Psychological Review*, 123(4), 349-367. | Their revision: passivity in response to prolonged aversive events is the default, unlearned response (linked to the dorsal raphe nucleus); what is learned is control, detected by the medial prefrontal cortex, which can then inhibit that default. Keep "the default response to long, uncontrollable bad events"; do not say "science proves you can break any chain". |
| "Psychologists who studied giving up for 50 years" (IG/FB) | Same two papers (1967 to 2016). | Fine as phrased; it refers to Maier and Seligman's own line of work. |

Caveats to keep in mind for replies: the 2016 revision is itself debated (e.g. a 2021 commentary in the *Journal of Psychological Science*, East China Normal University, argues the evidence does not rule out a learned component). The "elephant tied with a rope as a baby" story people will mention in comments is a folk parable, not a study.
