## Facts and sources

The reel is a parable and makes no factual claim on screen (the rope, the pit, the twin and the word DOUBT are story). The copy's science claims:

| Claim in the copy | Source | How to phrase it (do not overclaim) |
|---|---|---|
| "Self-handicapping: protecting yourself from failure by stacking the odds against you first." | Berglas, S. & Jones, E. E. (1978). Drug choice as a self-handicapping strategy in response to noncontingent success. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 36(4), 405-417. The term was coined here. | In the original study, men who got unexplained success on a test were more likely to choose a performance-impairing drug before the retest. It was a small lab study; later work has found self-handicapping in many everyday forms (procrastination, not practising). Say "one version of this", never "this is why you fail". |
| "People who meet mistakes with self-criticism tend to stay stuck; with self-compassion tend to try again." | Breines, J. G. & Chen, S. (2012). Self-compassion increases self-improvement motivation. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 38(9), 1133-1143. | Across four experiments, participants prompted to think about a failure or weakness with self-compassion showed more motivation to improve than those given a self-esteem boost or a neutral comparison. Keep "tend to"; these are lab experiments, mostly with university students. |
| "Beating yourself up doesn't make you try harder; it makes you more likely to quit." (IG/FB, shortened) | Same paper, plus Gilbert, P. & Procter, S. (2006). Compassionate mind training for people with high shame and self-criticism. *Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy*, 13(6), 353-379 (a pilot study of people with high self-criticism; compassion training reduced self-criticism and shame). | The Breines & Chen result is "self-compassion increased motivation", not "self-criticism makes people quit". The IG/FB line is a plain-language summary of the direction of the finding; if challenged, point to the paper and say "tends to". |
| "You can't win a tug of war against yourself. You can only let go of one end." | None: this is the reel's metaphor, not a finding. | Do not present it as science or as a quote from a study. |

Caveats for replies: self-compassion research is mostly self-report and lab-based, and the effect sizes vary between studies; "letting go" in the reel is about the inner critic, not about giving up on goals. Self-handicapping is a normal protective habit, not a character flaw.
