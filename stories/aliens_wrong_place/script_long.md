# What If We're Looking for Aliens in the Wrong Place?

## Cold open (0:00–0:30)
**[GFX]** Black. Radio static. A thin green line runs flat across the screen.

On the night of August 15th, 1977, a radio telescope in Ohio was listening to the sky.

**[GFX]** A printout of columns of characters scrolls by. One column lights up: 6 · E · Q · U · J · 5.

For seventy-two seconds, it heard something. A signal so strong and so narrow that, days later, the astronomer reading the printout circled it in red pen and wrote one word next to it.

**[GFX]** A red circle is drawn, then the handwritten "Wow!"

"Wow."

**[AI]** A night sky over a dark field.

It was never heard again.

**Title card:** WHAT IF WE'RE LOOKING FOR ALIENS IN THE WRONG PLACE?

## 1. Listening (0:30–1:05)
**[MOTION]** A radio dish under the Milky Way, slowly tracking.

The search began seventeen years earlier. In 1960, at Green Bank in West Virginia, a young astronomer named Frank Drake pointed an 85-foot radio dish at two nearby Sun-like stars, Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani, and listened.

**[GFX]** The radio spectrum, with a glowing band at 1420 MHz labelled "the hydrogen line".

He tuned to 1420 megahertz, the natural frequency of hydrogen, the most common element in the universe. If anyone out there wanted to be heard, the reasoning went, that's where they would call.

For four months, he heard nothing but static.

## 2. The silence (1:05–1:40)
**[AI]** A 1950s lunch table at Los Alamos, seen from behind, in warm window light.

Ten years before that, the physicist Enrico Fermi had asked a simple question over lunch: if the galaxy is so old and so vast… where is everybody?

**[GFX]** A timeline draws on: 1960 Ozma · 1977 Wow! · 2015 Breakthrough Listen · 2019 a "signal" from Proxima Centauri.

Since then we've listened with bigger dishes and better computers. In 2019, astronomers even caught a signal that seemed to come from our nearest neighbouring star.

**[GFX]** The Proxima signal is stamped "LOCAL INTERFERENCE".

It turned out to be our own electronics.

More than sixty years later, the answer is still silence.

## 3. The wrong place (1:40–2:25)
**[MOTION]** Earth's night side from orbit, city lights sliding past.

But what if we've been listening for the wrong thing?

Look at Earth from far away. We aren't sending the universe a message. But we are changing it. Our cities glow at night. Our machines leak radio into space. We've even altered the chemistry of our own air.

**[GFX]** Earth with three labelled layers: city lights · radio leakage · industrial gases in the atmosphere.

So scientists now search for technosignatures. Not a hello. Just evidence that someone, somewhere, built something. Artificial light on a distant planet. A gas in its atmosphere that nature doesn't make. Heat from machines.

**[AI]** A listener in headphones at an old monitor, seen from behind.

Not a voice calling to us. Just the traces a civilisation leaves behind, the way ours does.

## 4. Closer to home (2:25–3:05)
**[AI]** A night cockpit, a pilot's gloved hand, a distant light outside.

And then there's the question closer to home. For years, military pilots have reported things in the sky they couldn't identify.

**[GFX]** A grid of 144 dots; one turns grey, labelled "balloon".

In 2021, a US intelligence report reviewed 144 of those sightings. It could confidently explain only one: a large, deflating balloon.

**[GFX]** The grid starts resolving dot by dot: balloons · drones · clutter · sensor errors.

Later reviews explained many more. And in 2024, the Pentagon's own UAP office reviewed decades of records and found no evidence of alien technology.

Unexplained doesn't mean alien. It means unexplained.

## 5. How little we've searched (3:05–3:40)
**[GFX]** The key visual: Earth's oceans, then a single small hot tub.

So have we really looked?

In 2018, astronomers estimated how much of the possible search space all our efforts had covered. Compare the search to Earth's oceans, and everything we've ever done is about one hot tub of water.

**[GFX]** The camera pulls back from the hot tub to the ocean, until the tub is a dot.

If you scooped a single hot tub out of the ocean and found no fish… you wouldn't conclude the ocean is empty.

## Ending (3:40–4:00)
**[MOTION]** Deep space, a slow pull back, stars.

So no, there is no confirmed evidence that aliens are here.

But maybe the silence isn't an answer.

Maybe we just haven't learned how to listen.
