## Scientific guardrails

- Autobiographical/episodic remembering is **reconstructive**, not a literal playback of an intact video recording. The word "rebuilds" is a simple visual metaphor, not a claim that nothing is stored.
- Experiences involve distributed patterns of neural activity; encoding and recall involve changing and reactivating networks of neurons. The hippocampus helps bind elements of an episode and supports retrieval.
- A smell, sound, location or other familiar cue **can** help reactivate relevant memory representations. Not every cue recalls every memory.
- Recalling a memory can **sometimes** change it. Do **not** assert that every act of remembering must distort a memory, or that every retrieval always makes it inaccurate. The conditions under which reconsolidation/update occurs are actively researched.
- Red threads, branching forests and memory rooms are **illustrative visual metaphors**, not literal brain anatomy.
- Good sources: APA Dictionary of Psychology (reconstructive memory) https://dictionary.apa.org/reconstructive-memory ; neurobiological review of retrieval https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6903648/ ; review on reconstructive and episodic memory networks https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314160/ ; reconsolidation caveats https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247380/ .
