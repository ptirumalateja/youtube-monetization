## Part 1: Calm narration

Some stories are not about winning. They are about the moment someone realises they are alone.

The Mahabharata is full of such moments. On the field of Kurukshetra, the Pandavas and the Kauravas faced each other for eighteen days. Bhishma fell. Drona took command. And on the thirteenth day, Drona arranged his army into the Chakravyuha: a great wheel of warriors, ring inside ring, closing like a spiral.

Only a few knew how to break it. Arjuna knew. Krishna knew. But Arjuna had been drawn away by the Samshaptakas, far across the field.

So Yudhishthira turned to Abhimanyu, the son of Arjuna and Subhadra.

## Part 2: Dialogue and emotion (quieter, then firmer)

Abhimanyu said, quietly, "I can break in. I don't know the way out."

Yudhishthira answered, "Break it open. We will be right behind you. Bhima, Nakula, Sahadeva, all of us."

And for a moment, everyone believed it.

## Part 3: Urgent, faster

He charged. His chariot tore through the first ring. Then the second. Then the third. Arrows, dust, horses, shouting, and still he pushed deeper.

Then he turned around.

No one was there.

Jayadratha, king of the Sindhus, had sealed the gap. The whole Pandava army was stuck outside.

## Part 4: Slow, heavy, final

Karna broke his bow. Dushasana's son faced him. Duryodhana watched. His chariot was smashed, his sword was shattered.

So he picked up a wheel, and kept fighting.

He didn't die because he couldn't get out.

He died because no one could get in.
