## The 19.3-second screenplay

It uses four sets, one rig and one in-world counter: a tear-off day calendar (big numerals, one page per day, the kind on every Indian office desk). Expenses arrive the same way the salary did, as buzzes on his phone, so the cause is always visible and he is always on screen to react. Slots follow your reel structure: Hook, Lock-in, Body 1, Rehook 1, Body 2, Rehook 2, Close.

## Character animation and staging fixes

### One rig, one costume, start to finish

- Lock the design: cream shirt with rolled sleeves, navy trousers, dark shoes, short dark hair, crimson bag strap. He sleeps in the work shirt (which is also part of the story: he's too tired to change). The same createFigure options are used in every scene, imported from one HERO constant so no scene can pass its own colours.
- Proportions: keep the rig's lengths (L in sld.js) but grow the head to about 1/5.5 of his height, and give him a mitten hand (palm plus thumb) in place of the sphere, so you can see which way the hand faces.
- Extras: cut the landlord, clerk, vendor and co-worker. If the office needs life, use one co-worker who is always out of focus and gets a 2–3 s idle loop (typing, sipping, shifting weight). Nobody stands frozen.

### Why the hands go backward, and the fix (from sld.js)

- The elbow has no limit. In FK mode the forearm angle is sa+ea with ea unclamped, so any negative eL/eR (from a blend or the gait) bends the forearm the wrong way. Clamp e to [0.05, 2.5] rad and the shoulder swing to [−0.9, 2.6].
- The IK pole flips. When no pole is given, skel() uses E − mid(S, Hd), which comes from the FK elbow. When the target crosses the shoulder line, that vector flips and the elbow snaps behind the body. Always pass a fixed anatomical pole in body space, down, back and out: V(-0.35,-1,±0.45).
- The pole switches mid-blend. blend() sets o.p = b.p || a.p, which jumps from one pole to the other. Slerp the pole between the two poses.
- Reaches take the wrong path. Blending a target weight from 0 to 1 moves the hand in a straight line from the FK pose, which can pass through the torso. Give every reach an arc: lift via a mid-point in front of the chest, then go to the target.
- Add a QA assert that fails the render when an elbow bends backward: dot(cross(upper, fore), bodySide) < 0, or when a hand goes behind the torso plane outside an allowed window.

### Face and emotion

- Keep the canvas face, but make it read at phone size: eyes 1.4× bigger, brows 1.5× thicker, a 2-frame half-lid on every blink, and the face cap tilted 10° down when he looks at the phone.
- Use five named expressions and blend over 6–8 frames, never snap: SLEEP, JOY, CONTENT, FLAT, WORRY, HOLLOW. Map them to the story: 0–3 s JOY → 6 s CONTENT → 7–10 s FLAT/WORRY → 12 s HOLLOW → 18 s a JOY flash, then HOLLOW.
- Let the posture do most of the acting: spine curve, head pitch and shoulder height follow a single hope value (1.0 → 0.1 over the month), so he sags a little more every day without needing new keys.
- Light the emotional beats with the phone: a cool point light at his face whenever the phone is lit. It is the cause of every reaction and it separates the face from the warm room.

### Sleeping pose

- Lie him on his side in a light fetal curl: lower arm under the pillow, upper hand near the phone, knees bent about 60°, head turned 15° toward the camera so the face reads.
- Make the blanket a lofted mesh that drapes over the hip, knee and shoulder points of the skeleton (a few control points are enough), not a flat box. Add a breathing loop: chest and blanket rise 2% every 4 s.

### Getting out of bed (0.8 s = 24 frames at 30 fps)

```
f0   anticipation: shoulders curl in, blanket hand grips
f3   blanket thrown back (3-key cloth blend), torso starts to rise
f8   sitting, hips pivot on the mattress EDGE (constraint: pelvis y >= mattress top)
f11  legs swing over the edge, knees stay bent, feet travel in an arc
f14  feet plant (foot-lock until lift-off), both hands press the mattress (IK targets on the mattress)
f18  forward lean, nose over toes, weight onto the feet
f22  stand, 2-frame overshoot upward
f24  cut on action; arms finish settling in the next shot
```

### Walk

- Use a 1.0 s cycle for two steps (contact, down, passing, up), with feet locked while planted (no sliding) and the hip bobbing 2 cm.
- Arms swing opposite the legs. The bag follows the hip with a 2-frame delay and a damped spring, which is what makes the walk look physical.
- Morning and night use the same cycle with different parameters: morning has full stride, head up and arm swing 0.22; night has 70% stride, head down 0.3 rad and arm swing 0.06.

### Staging rules

- He faces what he is doing, and the camera sees his face on every reaction. Never shoot an emotional beat from behind.
- Wide shots are for place and time (the street, the time-lapse), close shots are for feeling (waking, ₹0, the false hope). Your "small man, lots of room" look stays on the wide shots only.
- One overlay at a time, and it never covers his face. Cards sit in the empty part of the frame, on the side away from where he is looking.
- Use hard cuts on action between sets, with no dissolves (the ghosting at 4.0 s and 17.4 s goes away).
