## Facts used (keep identical everywhere)
| On screen | Fact |
|---|---|
| 60 KM BLACK HOLE (stellar-mass, ~10 suns) | Event-horizon diameter = 5.906 km x mass in suns, so ~10 suns is ~59 km, about 60 km: roughly a city. Typical stellar-mass range is ~5-20 suns. |
| 430,000x (Sagittarius A*) | ~4.3 million suns: horizon about 25 million km across, 0.17 AU. 25 million / 59 = ~430,000. About 18 Suns side by side fit across it; it fits inside Mercury's orbit. |
| 1,500x (M87*) | ~6.5 billion suns: horizon about 38 billion km (~257 AU) across. 6.5e9 / 4.3e6 = ~1,500. Wider than Neptune's whole orbit (about 60 AU across), by ~4x. |
| 10x (TON 618) | Up to ~66 billion suns (published estimates run ~40-66 billion, always say "up to"): horizon about 390 billion km (~2,600 AU) across. 66 / 6.5 = ~10. |
| 43x wider than this (hook B) | TON 618's horizon (~390 billion km) / Neptune's orbit across (~9 billion km, ~60 AU) = ~43. |
| LIGHT NEEDS 15 DAYS TO CROSS THIS | 3.9e11 km / 299,792 km/s = 1.3 million s = ~15 days. The Solar System out to Neptune's orbit takes light about 8 hours. |
- Always say "event horizon" (not "size of the black hole"), "to Neptune's orbit", "up to ~66 billion", never "the biggest" or "king" (the record is contested and the mass is an estimate). What we see around TON 618 is the quasar glow, not the black hole.
- Thought experiment, not to scale: the Solar System and planets are drawn bigger than true size and the opening is compressed; the ladder keeps only the ratios above. Visuals are AI-assisted (procedural) and 3D-rendered.
